## 5. Decisions and doubts

- **GO is the wheel's hub**, not a big button under the game: the press that starts the attempt is already the hand on the wheel, and the circuit gets the button's 62 px (it is only 247 by 272 at 360x640 as it is). The hub is 71 px across at 360 wide; the wheel is always drawn, so nothing moves when GO goes.
- **The frame is 100 by 110**, a little taller than wide: on a tall phone (390x844) it fills the width exactly (366 by 403); on a short one (360x640) the height binds and it is 247 by 272. A taller frame would only have shrunk it on both.
- **The wheel's foot dips under the strip's edge** on short phones (a dashboard's look): at 360x640 the wheel is 155 px across (46% of the game's width) in a strip 129 px tall; its mark stays in sight at full lock (100 degrees).
- **A drag anywhere on the game turns the wheel** once the attempt is on (the standing rule: inputs can start anywhere), not only on the strip; the wheel's drawing turns either way. The drag is relative: it turns the wheel on from where it stood when taken.
- **One circuit a square** for both players and every attempt (before: a new road for each attempt and each player). Players learn it; the miniature shows it.
- **The circuits are loops with a few kinks, not hairpin circuits**: at a road 12 wide in a frame of 100 with corners no tighter than 12 to 13 there is no room for more (a narrower road would make the car too small on a 360 px phone: it is 15 px long there now). The generator keeps the bendiest of five that pass.
- **No Go again on success switch** (the game had none; the task named no meaning for it). **No attempt time limit setting**: the car's speed is fixed, so a lap's time is too; the internal cap is 1.6 laps' worth.
- **The lap's progress bar is gone**: the car on the circuit is the progress (the task: "not needed beyond the lap's progress").
- **Laps are not kept** when an old square is dealt again (`stale` goes through `newMini` in `core/records.js`; keeping them would have meant touching it).
- **Kerbs are blue and white**, the miniature's car white: red and yellow are the players' alone.
- **Balance by the computer's lapses only**: Normal finishes about a third of its laps on the default mix. The computer's lapse is a wheel held still, so it is harmless on a plain oval and deadly on a twisty circuit: on `gentle` Normal laps 64% and squares run 2.2 turns (first mover 64%); on the default `mixed` and on `twisty` it is in range. If `gentle` is ever made the default, raise `aiLapse`.
- **Hard v Hard** runs 2.3 turns with the first mover on 66%: the usual shape of a race where both finish in the same number of turns.
- **My scripted "human"** (real pointer events on the page, Playwright's mouse and CDP touches): it reads the car's place from the page, steers for a point 0.45 s ahead, sees it 140 ms late on top of a 35 to 40 ms loop (about 180 ms in all) with a little noise, and drags to the wheel angle that wants. Careful: 4 laps of 4 at 360x640 (road 11, speed 30, the first defaults), then 2 of 3 at 390x844 and 1 of 3 by touch under a heavily loaded machine at the final defaults (road 12, speed 26). Careless (260 ms late, three times the noise, lapses): 0 of 4, each off within the first bends. A hand that only reacts, 180 ms late with a 0.28 s look ahead, swings wider and wider and is off at the first bend: the game punishes steering late, as it should, but a first-time player will crash a few times before the wheel's lag is learnt. A model of the same hand in Node says a lap is safe up to about 250 ms of lag and falls off a cliff at 300 ms; speed 26 and road 12 were chosen for that margin (at speed 30 and road 11 the model lost 14% of laps at 250 ms). Real thumbs should decide `speed`, `turn` and `travel`: they are settings.
- **The pointer capture on `#app`** at GO is there because the pressed button is redrawn away under the finger; tested with mouse and CDP touch events in Chromium, not on a real iPhone.
- The move's entry carries the steering (about 1.3 KB for a lap, 2000 entries at the very most) on each of the record's recent entries, as Brick Break's log does.
- `npm test` under the machine's load (six builders at once, load average 40) failed only tests that time other games' computers ("under 60 ms", "quickly") and the three that need the database emulator ("no database emulator"); see the final message for the last run.
