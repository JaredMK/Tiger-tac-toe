## 1. CLAUDE.md paragraph

- Letter Hive (`hive`, December 2026, Admin only, brain, `realTime`, `turnSecs: 60`; `CLIENT_VERSION` and its `minVersion` 1.30.0): seven letters in a honeycomb, one of them the centre, dealt from the game's own puzzles (`mini.pz`, the puzzle's number; `hive/puzzles.js`, 58 puzzles, 3,103 words, 13.6 KB, written by `scripts/make-hive-puzzles.js path/to/enable1.txt` from ENABLE, the full list so words up to 15 letters count; the offensive words left out exactly as Word Tiles' script leaves them out; each puzzle its letters, the centre first, a colon and its words prefix-coded, `rules.js` `hivePuzzle(i)` → `{ letters, centre, words, set, full, plain }` decoded on first use). The puzzles are our own: the script's `PICKS` is a hand-picked list of everyday words of seven different letters, each naming its letter set, and the script works out the rest and stops on a pick that breaks the curation rules (no S, at most one of J Q X Z, a word with all seven letters, the centre the letter whose list is nearest 48 words among those with 36 to 66 words and 200 points or more, 4 times the highest target, no two puzzles on the same letters, 15 everyday words worth 60 points at least); `COMMON` in the script is the everyday words among the puzzles' words, marked by hand (1,165; the rest of ENABLE's are fair to enter but few would think of them), written into the data as a capital (`pz.plain`): the computer leans to them. A word is 4 letters or more, of the hive's letters alone (a letter as often as wanted), with the centre letter in it. A move is one burst of `secs` 20 seconds played on the mover's phone, `{ w: [words…] }` in the order found (lower case, `HIVE.cap` 20 at most; anything that is not an object with an array of strings is refused): each word that is in the puzzle's list and that nobody has found (the other player, an earlier burst, earlier in this one) scores, anything else is dropped without a word (never scored, the move not refused for it); 4 letters score `four` 1, a longer word its length, a word with all seven letters (a "full hive" word) `bonus` 7 more; a word found is gone for both (`found` every word in the order found, `by` a letter a word for who found it, `pts { X, O }`, `n` bursts each, `last { by, w, pts, full, from, to }`); the burst stops counting at the word that takes its finder to `target` 40 and that player takes the square (the last word's points are kept, so a score can pass the target); an empty burst is a move; every word found (it cannot happen at these sizes): the higher score, level a draw (note `out`/`tie`). Nothing is hidden (the list ships with the page) and how many words a phone found in its time is the phone's word, so it is **held out of Find Match** (`FIND_MATCH_HOLD`). "Go again on success" (off): a burst with a full-hive word in it plays another (`again`; `heldSub` "[name] filled the hive · goes again"). Settings (`TUNE`): `again`, `secs` (Timing), `target` (10 to 50), `four`, `bonus` (Rules), and the computer's (Computer): `aiRare` 20 (an unusual word is that likely beside an everyday one, %), per level the words a burst (`aiMin`/`aiMax` 1 to 4, Hard 2 to 5, Easy 1 to 3) and the lean to short words (`aiShort` 45, Hard 50, Easy 35: a word a letter longer is that likely, %), `aiFullHard` 10 (Hard goes looking for a full-hive word, % of bursts). The view (`hive/view.js`, DOM and one SVG, no canvas): the two tallies (`.ctals.two`), under each its player's found words as chips in a box of whole rows (`.hvlist`, 2 rows on a short phone to 5 on a tall one, `--hvr`; it scrolls inside itself, kept at its end, a full-hive word inked with a star), the time bar (`.hvbar`), the word being made (`.hvent`, the centre letter blue, set smaller past 11 letters), the reserved line (`.hvline`), the hive (`hiveSvg`: seven flat-topped hexagon cells, each a shadow, a top and its letter, the centre cell the sea's blue, never a player's colour; sized from the room left, `.hvbox`), and the buttons: Go! before a burst, Delete / Shuffle / Enter during it. The burst is on `ui/round.js` `makeRound` with the view's own frame loop (`loop`: the clock, the bar, the line's say): a press on a cell adds its letter, taken on the press itself (`pointerdown`, so two thumbs can type; `hive-del`/`hive-shuf`/`hive-ent` only answer a click with no press before it); Enter checks the word at once (`rules.js` `hiveWhy`: "Too short", "Needs the centre letter", "Not a word", "Already found", the letters going with a shake) or takes it (its chip pops into the list, the tally bumps, "+5" in the line; a full-hive word waves the hive, "Full hive! +14"); Shuffle re-arranges the outer letters on this phone only (`lay`); a keyboard types, Backspace deletes, Enter enters, Space shuffles; during the burst the page is changed in place (`paint`, no redraw: a redraw would cut a press short), and a redraw draws the same from the burst's state; the burst goes in as it stands at its time, when Board is pressed (a capture listener ends it first, its result shows, then the board comes by itself), 900 ms before the turn's clock online, and `HIVEV.last` 550 ms after the word that reaches the target; online its words stay on show until the record lands (`sent`, at most `HIVEV.sent` 8 s); this phone's own flash goes straight to its result (`skipFlash`). Anyone else's burst (the computer's too) is never streamed (`replay.synth` gives none): its words land in their finder's list one after another (`rules.js` `HIVE`: `pre` 300, `beat` 480 a word, a long burst squeezed into `span` 2,880, `tail` 300, `hold` 700; `hiveWordAt`, `hiveShowMs`, inside `flashMs`), the list scrolled along as each lands (`follow`), the points counting on through them, then the line ("3 words · +11", "Full hive! 2 words · +19", "No words"). Sounds `hvGo`/`hvKey`/`hvDel`/`hvShuf`/`hvWord`/`hvFull`/`hvNo`/`hvEnd`/`hvLand` (this phone's own inputs buzz: tap, medium for a word, success for a full hive, error for a refusal). The miniature (`.thvw`): the little honeycomb with the seven letters, the centre cell blue, beside each player's points as a bar (`barRows`). The computer (`ai.js`) never enters more than a person could: a number of words by its level, drawn from the words not yet found by their weights (short and everyday first), Hard sometimes a full-hive word; nothing searched; `needs` the points still needed over a Normal burst's worth on the words left (`hiveBurst`). `tests/sim/hive.sim.js`: Normal v Normal 4.6 bursts each (median 4.5; the winner's 4.9), the first mover 56.7% over 4,000 squares (53.2% over the asserted 500), no square won in a first burst and 0.5% in a second; 35 points 4.1 bursts, 45 5.1; Normal at 2 to 4 words 3.9 bursts and 60.5% (why its range is 1 to 4); Hard v Hard 3.0 bursts, 60.6%; Easy v Easy 6.1; Hard beats Normal about 9 in 10. ## 5. Decisions and doubts

- **Puzzles are hand-picked, rule-checked.** 58 puzzles, each named by an everyday seven-letter-set word I chose from the 3,892 letter sets that pass the rules (continue, auction, kitchen, rainbow, thunder…). Two picks were dropped for too few everyday words (manhole, holiday). Words run 36 to 66 a puzzle, 202 to 384 points.
- **Everyday words marked by hand (not asked for).** ENABLE has no frequency data and the repo has no clean-licensed one, so "common-looking" would have been a guess by letters. I read the 2,611 distinct words of the puzzles and marked 1,165 as everyday (in the generator, `COMMON`); the data carries the mark and the computer leans to them 5 to 1 (`aiRare`). It is my judgement of English, with the mistakes that implies; it only affects what the computer finds and one curation rule. A person may enter any word of the list.
- **Normal finds 1 to 4 words, not the 2 to 4 suggested**: with 2 to 4 the first mover won 60 to 63% at every target (every burst worth about the same). Hard is 2 to 5 (not 4 to 6): at 4 to 6 it won in under 2 bursts each. Easy 1 to 3.
- **The score can pass the target** (the winning word's points are kept: "43/40"); the gauge and the miniature's bar stop at full.
- **Hard v Hard: the first mover 60.6%** (3 bursts each): over the 60% line by a hair; not asserted, said in the sim's header.
- **The computer's later bursts are worth more than its first** (the short everyday words go first), the opposite of a person. Not harmful, but a person facing the computer late in a square sees it find longer words.
- **Board pressed mid-burst ends the burst in place** (its result shows, then the board comes) rather than leaving with the burst lost: the brief asked for "goes in as it stands" and `attemptMove` needs the square open. Leaving by the menu (quitting the match) loses the burst as in the other timed-round games.
- **A burst that cannot be sent is lost** (online, the line down at the moment it ends): as in Fruit Slice and Rhythm Tap (`ui/round.js`).
- **Online, own burst**: I could not run two phones on the emulators here; the path where this phone's words stay on show until the server's record lands (`sent`) is reasoned and covered only by `npm run test:flows` (online with the engine as the server: passes), not seen on a real server.
- **Tap targets**: a cell is 71x62 px at 360x640, 78x67 at 390x660, 109x95 at 390x844; at 320x568 (under the floor) about 47x41.
- **No points on the chips**: the points show in the line and the tally as a word lands; a chip is the word alone (two or three fit a row at 360 px).
- **Typing a letter that is not in the hive on a keyboard is ignored silently.**
- `bonus` at 0 takes a few puzzles' totals just under 4 x 50 (the rule is checked at the default scoring).
- **`npm test` is not green on this machine, for a reason that is not this game's**: the last full run (with the six builds running at once, load average 35 to 40) was 1,622 tests, 1,607 passed, 14 failed, every one of them another game's wall-clock assertion on its Hard computer ("within its time", "quickly", "under 60 ms": Backgammon Lite, 21, Ride the Bus, Four in a Row, Clash Grid, Districts, Hangman x2, Hex, Risky Rolls, Peg Drop, Poker Hands, Thirty-One, Five Letters); an earlier run at load 18 failed 8 of the same kind, a different set.
