## 5. Decisions and doubts

- **Every side of every piece is cut, the sheet's outer sides too** (no flat edges; the picture has a 30-unit margin so outer knobs show a little more of it). With classic flat edges every 3 by 3 piece but the centre is an edge piece and its shape alone names its cell (a corner has two flat sides, each edge-middle one): the picture would not matter and the game would be 4-4 every time. The outline still helps honestly: a piece's knob must meet the socket of the piece it lies beside. When the square is decided the pieces fade into the whole picture as a plain framed square.
- **"Never shown before it is complete"** is read as "before the square is decided". Five of eight wins with pieces still in the pile, so on the deciding placement the pile's pieces drop in and then the cuts fade: every square ends on the whole picture (a win and a 4-4 alike). A finished square looked at again shows the whole picture with a small mark on each piece for who laid it, and no tray.
- `offer` is null before a draw, as decided; `placed` is a string rather than a list (a list with gaps does not survive the Realtime Database), read through `jigCells`. `by` (who drew) and `k` (pieces drawn) are added: `timeoutClose` needs the first, the phone's timer key the second.
- The tray's piece is shown at about a board piece's size (larger on a 360 wide phone, the same at 390x844), not much larger: the board, the tray, the tallies and the line all have to fit 360x640.
- The timer bar runs up the tray's side rather than under the piece (it left the piece 25% more room).
- **The computer's model** ("right with a chance by level and by how many empty cells"): I count the places a piece could actually go (empty cells that touch the board), not all empty cells: that is what a player chooses between. Chance = the level's share at four or more places, rising to certain at one. A flat chance would give slightly fewer draws (52% against 57% at 75%) but is less like a person.
- **The piece timer never fires unless its bar is on the page** (`app.querySelector('.jgtime.on')`). Needed for `tests/ui/own-move-wait.test.mjs`, whose phones run 30 s of mock clock between games with no DOM: the timer ran out there and sent an `'out'` the harness never answers, which broke the next game's check. It is a fair rule in itself (the timer is what the player is shown) but it is a guard found by a test, so worth a look.
- **Doubt: how hard it is for a person.** I cannot measure it. Rows of the sheet are easy to tell (sky, horizon, ground); columns need a look at what runs on from the neighbour. If people are right nearly always, expect draws nearly always; the timer and `centre` are the admin's levers.
- **Doubt: the piece timer is the phone's word**, as the task says; a reload does not reset it (kept in local storage), but a changed client could ignore it. Held out of Find Match.
- Not done: the other phone does not see the mover's timer (only the piece, then where it went); no tick is played for someone else's piece. Nothing is streamed live (`net/live.js` untouched): the draw and the placement each show as they land.
- 4 by 4 and 5 by 5 work and are tested, but run 11 and 17 turns each: the size setting is there, the default is the only one inside the length target.
- **Tests under load.** With the other builders' sims running (load average 25 on 4 cores) five time-budget tests of other games failed in my full `npm test` runs: `clash` ("under 60 ms"), `hangman` Hard and race v2 ("quickly"), `hex` Hard ("within its time"), `hunt` Hard ("quickly"). None touches this game; `hex` passed when run again alone. Everything else passes, the flows too.
