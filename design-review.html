Menu design review

Tic Tac Chaos, staging, 2026-11-12. A review of the menus and screens around the game, three directions for making them feel crafted, and mock-ups of each beside what is live today. Nothing in the game has changed: this page is not linked from it, and every mock-up is a static picture built for this page alone.

1Critique

The game has a real identity: the CHAOS! burst, the two characters, ink outlines, the hand-drawn # board. The menus use that identity for the logo and then stop. Below the logo, most screens are a beige page with white rounded boxes and stacked full-width buttons, which is the layout any generated or template app arrives at. The app icon is the sharpest contrast: nine loud patterned patches, none of which appear anywhere in the menus.

Main menu

  • Flat field. One beige (#f4e6c8) from edge to edge; about 40% of the screen is empty above and below the stack.
  • Dead slab in prime position. "Find Match · Not available at this time" is the third-largest thing on the screen and does nothing.
  • Only two colours at work (red, yellow). The icon's blue, green, pink and orange are absent.
  • Five buttons, one style. Size is the only hierarchy; nothing tells a new player what each mode is.

Play Computer setup

  • A form. "YOU PLAY AS:" and "DIFFICULTY" in 11px caps over rows of identical pills.
  • Placeholder faces. Both players show a dashed "?" circle even though X and O characters exist.
  • Dead gap of about 280px between Mini-games and Start match.
  • Yellow means two things: the selected option and the primary button.

Mini-games checklist

  • Names cut off: "FOUR IN A R…", "HIGHER/LO…".
  • "?" bubbles overlap the corner of every row and compete with the red checkboxes.
  • Thin line icons (2px strokes) next to chunky ink lettering: they read as a stock icon set, not as this game's drawing hand.

Host a room

  • Subtitle wraps to two lines beside the title.
  • Orphaned "?" on its own line under MINUTES.
  • Red helper text "Start opens once your friend has joined" looks like an error; the disabled Start above it already says it.
  • The room code lettering is strong and should stay.

Join a room

  • Default text fields on an otherwise empty page; Join sits 450px below them.
  • The code field could be five big letter boxes in the room code's own lettering.

Find Match, searching

  • Three small things on an empty screen for what may be a minute's wait.
  • Nothing to read or do while waiting, and no hint of how long it might take.
  • The one screen with no way back at the top, which is right, but it also loses its header rhythm.

My Stats

  • Reads as a dashboard: small grey labels ("wins-losses-draws · 30 games") in a white card.
  • Delete account is as large and loud as Log out, in red display type.
  • An empty third row in Top opponents.

Settings

  • A third toggle style: black-filled pills here, yellow-filled segments in setup, red checkboxes in the checklist.
  • Labels in the body font at one size; the page has no character at all.

How to Play

  • Three paragraphs in a box, then a list of 17 identical rows.
  • "TAP ONE FOR ITS RULES" in 10px display caps is hard to read.
  • The rules are simple enough to draw: pick a square, win its game, three in a row.

In-game bar and menu

  • The computer is a stock-looking monitor glyph beside two hand-drawn characters.
  • Menu card: three button widths in three rows; Resume and Main menu have equal weight.
  • The pink turn bar and "YOUR TURN" work.

Results

  • Ragged buttons: Rematch, View board, Main menu and Share result at four widths on three rows.
  • Little happens after the burst lands.
  • On a loss the headline (two lines, with the monitor glyph) is bigger than the win's.

Explainer

  • Works: PLAY / WIN chips, one Practice button.
  • "MORE BRAIN" is a 10px label; "Tap anywhere to close" is filler.

What reads as generic, across screens

  • White rounded card on beige, everywhere. Setup, stats, How to Play, the host room and the explainer all use the same box.
  • One button shape for every job. Mode choices, toggles, list rows and navigation are all the same outlined pill at different widths.
  • Three selection styles (yellow segment, black pill, red checkbox) for one idea.
  • Display type used small. Luckiest Guy at 10–11px for labels loses its shape; body text in grey (#6b5a45) on beige is low-contrast.
  • Instructional copy. "Not available at this time", "Enter the code the host shared", "Start opens once your friend has joined", "Tap anywhere to close".
  • Hard cuts between screens. The board-to-square zoom is lovely; menu to setup to match is an instant swap.
  • Thin stock-style icons for the mini-games and a monitor glyph for the computer.
  • Bottom-pinned buttons with empty middles on some screens, centred stacks on others.

What already works and should stay

  • The logo. Hopping TIC TAC letters over the wiggling CHAOS! burst. It is the brand.
  • X and O as characters with blinking eyes. Use them more: they should react.
  • Ink outlines with a hard drop shadow. Buttons already feel pressable.
  • The hand-drawn # board and the miniatures on it.
  • The WINNER! burst and the "You are X" reveal card.
  • The room code lettering on the host screen.
  • Luckiest Guy as the display face at 18px and up.
  • The Patchwork icon. It is the most distinctive thing the game owns.

2Benchmark

What well-loved casual and party games tend to do in their menus, as patterns to adapt rather than looks to copy. (Reference points: living-room party packs, obstacle-course battle royales, couch co-op cooking games, daily word puzzles, language-learning streak apps.) Every mock-up below is an original design in this game's own drawing hand.

The menu is a place, not a list

  • Modes are doors, tiles or cards with a picture each, on a background that belongs to the game.
  • For us: the menu as a quilt or a board, since the game itself is a grid of squares.

One loud colour idea

  • A full-bleed colour or pattern per screen, not a neutral page with accents.
  • For us: the icon's patterned patches, or X-red against O-yellow.

Buttons you want to press

  • Chunky, tilted a degree or two, they squash under the thumb and spring back with a sound.
  • One clear primary per screen, two to three times the size of the rest.
  • For us: the ink outline and hard shadow are halfway there already.

Characters that react

  • Mascots idle, look at what you touch, cheer and sulk.
  • For us: X and O already blink. They could face off in setup, gasp at a win and sulk at a loss.

Motion between screens

  • Menus arrive in a stagger; screens wipe, flip or zoom in the brand's own shape.
  • For us: the board zoom exists; a patch flip or burst wipe would carry it to the menus.

Sound on every touch

  • A family of short pitched ticks, a whoosh on navigation, a stinger on Start.
  • For us: one voice per direction (tap the mock-up buttons below to hear them).

Short, voiced copy

  • Two or three words per button; personality in waits and empty states.
  • For us: "Meet a rival", "Same again?", rotating quips while searching.

Results as a moment

  • A big reaction, one or two facts as badges, one obvious next step, a share card.
  • For us: the burst and board are right; add reaction, facts and a tidy action row.

3Three directions

All three keep the logo, the two characters, ink outlines, the # board and Luckiest Guy for headlines. They differ in what fills the page around them.

A · Patchwork

The app icon becomes the menu.

Idea
Every screen is a quilt of patterned patches divided by ink. Each way to play is a patch.
Palette
The icon's nine, on ink.
Type
Luckiest Guy on cream label plates; Fredoka for small text (new, replaces Patrick Hand in menus).
Buttons
Patches with a tilted cream label. Selected: a yellow stitched frame and a tick.
Icons
Chunky filled drawings on cream tiles; luck and brain games colour-coded.
Motion
Patches pop in one by one; stripes drift; patch confetti on a win. Screens change by flipping a patch.
Sound
Soft pitched thumps, a different note per patch, a rising run on Start.
Copy
Short and friendly: "You vs the machine", "Meet a rival".

B · Sticker book

Today's paper look, made by hand.

Idea
The beige page becomes a scrapbook: die-cut stickers, tape, ruled paper, pen circles, margin doodles.
Palette
Today's tokens plus sticker white.
Type
Luckiest Guy on stickers; Patrick Hand, larger and in ink, as handwriting. No new fonts.
Buttons
Stickers with a white edge, each tilted its own way. Selected: circled in red pen.
Icons
The same chunky drawings, as round stickers.
Motion
Stickers slap down in turn; small ones rock; the pen circle draws itself. Screens turn like pages.
Sound
Paper slaps, a pencil tick, a peel.
Copy
Handwritten asides: "same again?", "go on, tap it".

C · Comic chaos

The CHAOS! burst takes over.

Idea
X-red against O-yellow, halftone dots, turning rays, skewed panels, speech bubbles.
Palette
Two team colours, ink, white, one cyan accent.
Type
Luckiest Guy logo; BANGERS FOR SHOUTS; Comic Neue for captions (two new fonts).
Buttons
Skewed panels with a hard shadow and a caption tag. Selected: inverted to ink and yellow.
Icons
The same drawings in white discs on cards; burst-shaped badges.
Motion
Rays turn; headlines punch in; panels slide from alternate sides; VS and Start pulse.
Sound
Punchy blips and a "bam" on Start.
Copy
Announcer and bubbles: "Who's playing today?", "Rematch?!".

4bPatchwork v2

Round 2 of direction A, mock-ups only. Its look stays; what changes is that you can always tell what to tap. A first pass asked, on each screen, "could a first-time player tell within two seconds what they can tap?" and A failed it in places: it used the same cream label plate for buttons, for facts ("3 IN A ROW", "9 MOVES") and for plain labels ("YOU", "PICK"), and left some real buttons (View board, Menu, the account, Privacy) with none. v2 has one button language, one information language and one selected marker, used on every screen, and adds Play a Friend, Host, Join, My Stats and Settings. Direction B's steps 1 to 4 are live on staging meanwhile; nothing here touches the game.

Button

  • A raised cream plate: ink outline, hard ink shadow, a slight tilt, Luckiest Guy.
  • Pressed: the patch lifts, the plate squashes down and loses its shadow, with the patch's note.
  • A whole patch you can tap always carries a plate. Nothing else ever does.
  • At least 44 pt, often far more.

Information

three in a rowyou lead 4–2
42wins
  • Flat: no outline, no shadow, no tilt.
  • Labels and facts on a tan ribbon with notched ends; values printed straight on a patch.
  • Never cream plate styling; nothing moves when it is touched.
  • Unavailable things (Find Match while it is off) are information too, greyed, with a ribbon saying why.

Selected

  • One marker for every choice: a yellow stitched frame with ink either side and a tick.
  • Only on the chosen option of a group; the stitches run slowly round, the tick pops in as it is picked.
  • The option keeps its plate: it is still a button.

Decorative

  • Pattern alone: no words, no plate, no shadow.
  • Fills the quilt between things; never moves when touched.

Tap anything in a mock-up to feel its press and hear it. Each screen is shown as it moves, then annotated: every tappable area outlined in blue and numbered (motion stopped, information dimmed). Checked in a browser: 67 tappable areas over the nine screens, every one at least 44 by 44 pt and every one carrying a plate; no plate on anything that is not tappable; nothing spills out of its patch.

5Recommendation

A · Patchwork, with two borrowings from B: the hand-tilted labels and the "slap" entrance. It is the only direction that nobody else could ship, because it comes from the game's own icon and its own premise (a grid where every square is something to play). It brings in the colours the menus lack, gives every screen a clear structure with no empty middle, and it scales: a new mode or setting is one more patch. B is the safe fallback if three weeks is too much now: about half the work, no new font, and a real improvement, but it stays closest to what is there. C is the most exciting single screen and the riskiest system: it leans hardest on two new fonts and a red and yellow field that fights the cream paper of the mini-games you land in a tap later.

Highest impact first, whichever direction you pick:

  1. Main menu as mode tiles, with Find Match reduced to a tag while it is off. medium
  2. Results screen: tidy action layout with Rematch dominant, facts as badges, a celebration that keeps moving. medium
  3. Setup: real characters, one selection style, Start as the one big action. medium
  4. Copy pass and menu sounds. The cheapest personality available. small
  5. How to Play as three drawn steps and a grid of games. medium
  6. Screen transitions between menus. medium
  7. Chunky mini-game icons, starting with the 17 released games. large
  8. The remaining screens (friend, join, host, searching, stats, settings, in-game menu) brought into the system. large

Items 1 to 4 are a week to a week and a half and would change how the game feels on first open. Nothing changes in the game until you choose.
